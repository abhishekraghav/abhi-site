A little about me

I've spent my career on the systems that can't afford to be wrong.

I'm a cybersecurity architect who's spent 10+ years designing and reviewing security for large-scale payments and infrastructure systems — the kind where a design flaw shows up as fraud, downtime or a regulator's phone call.

That work taught me most security failures trace back to an architecture decision made early, under time pressure, without the right person in the room. I try to be that person — early, specific, and focused on what will actually happen when someone attacks the system, not what a framework says should happen.

  • Background in cybersecurity architecture at a large payments organization
  • Focus on design-stage review — before controls get expensive to change
  • Works directly with engineering and architecture teams, not just security

Before we talk

Common questions

What kind of systems do you work on?

Mostly payments, identity and other systems handling regulated or high-value data — but the review process applies to any system where the design matters more than the checklist.

Do you work with my existing security team?

Yes — I work alongside your security and engineering teams rather than replacing them, and I hand off findings in a form they can act on directly.

What happens on the first call?

You describe the system or concern, I ask about the parts that usually hide risk, and we agree on whether an audit or ongoing advisory is the better fit.

What do I get at the end of an engagement?

A written architecture review, a threat model where relevant, and a prioritized list of fixes — not just a slide deck of findings.
